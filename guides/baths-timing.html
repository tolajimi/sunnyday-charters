When to go to The Baths

Morning light, a full mooring field, and the days you skip it.

The Baths is the granite corner of Virgin Gorda — house-sized boulders, a swim through the caves, Devil’s Bay on the far side. It is also a small national park. Timing is the whole difference between a quiet swim and a queue of snorkels.

The hour that matters

Arrive before nine if you can. The day-use moorings are first-come. By late morning in season they are gone, and the caves fill with day boats and the shore path from Spanish Town. After three the field thins again. The light is flatter then, but you will have the water.

On a sailing week we like a Sunday morning. Saturday is turnaround at the marinas. The outgoing boats have left; the new weeks have not yet reached Virgin Gorda. It is the same park. It does not feel like the same park.

When the sea says no

A north swell in winter can close the landing. The National Parks Trust flies a red flag when mooring and swimming are off. Purple means marine-life caution. We do not push a tender onto that beach for a photograph. Savannah Bay, Spring Bay, or a quiet Norman Island snorkel is the better morning. The caves will still be there on Thursday.

Hours and the small fee

Rangers are typically on the ground from mid-morning through mid-afternoon. Posted hours have sat around 8:30 or 9:00 until 4:00 or 4:30 — confirm the morning you go. Entry is a few dollars an adult, less for a child; BVI belongers walk in with ID. Crew on a private day or a week handles the park ticket and the mooring permit. You do not need to think about it unless you arrive by taxi from Spanish Town, in which case cash at the gate is still the simple way.

There is no overnight on those balls. You visit, you swim, you move. Spanish Town, Savannah Bay, or the North Sound is the night.

Season

December through April is the clearest water and the fullest park. May, early June, and November keep the warmth and drop a lot of the day-trip traffic. Mid-summer is hotter on the rocks and quieter in the caves. We would still go early.

What to bring, what to leave

Water shoes. The granite is wet and sharp. Reef-safe sunscreen — it is the rule in the park, and it is kinder to the coral you will swim over. A shirt for the walk through the boulders. Leave shells, coral, and drones. The trail through the caves is not lit. Be off it before dusk.
