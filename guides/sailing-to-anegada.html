Sailing to Anegada

The flat island, the reef you do not cross, and the forecast that decides the week.

Anegada is the outlier of the British Virgin Islands — coral, not volcanic, so low you barely see it until you are on it. The beaches are why guests ask. The reef is why some weeks never go.

The crossing

From the North Sound on Virgin Gorda the run is on the order of a dozen nautical miles north. On a sailing cat in a decent trade that is a couple of hours. On a powerboat it is shorter. You want good light for the arrival, not a late-day guess at the channel.

How you actually arrive

Horseshoe Reef wraps the southeast side. It is a protected fisheries zone. You do not fish it. You do not anchor on it. You do not take a shortcut across it because another boat did. Approach Setting Point through the buoyed channel only, in daylight, on a current chart. The inner marks are there for a reason.

Setting Point itself is shallow — often eight to eleven feet over sand. Plenty of charter cats go in. A deeper keel needs a careful ball or stays out and tenders. Some fleets simply will not release a bareboat to Anegada. That is a draft and insurance rule, not a mood.

Once you are in

Moorings and sand both exist. The field can feel tight when it is busy. Ashore at Setting Point you can eat that night if you order lobster by mid-afternoon — kitchens cook to a count, not to a walk-in at seven. The north shore beaches (Loblolly, Cow Wreck) are a taxi or a long walk. The salt ponds hold flamingos on a good day. None of that is a reason to rush the channel.

When we skip it

A leftover north swell, poor visibility, a forecast that will trap you overnight in an easterly slop, or a hull that does not belong in eight feet of water. The week then stays in the Drake Channel and the North Sound. Guests remember the water they actually swam. They do not remember the island they argued about on a chart.
