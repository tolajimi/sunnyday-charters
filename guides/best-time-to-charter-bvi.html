Best time to charter the BVI

Trade-wind winter, quieter shoulders, and the two holiday weeks that book first.

The British Virgin Islands sail year-round. The week you want depends on wind, crowd, and price — not on a single “best month” in a brochure.

December through April — peak

This is the classic season: easterly trades, dry-season sky, water in the mid-to-high 70s Fahrenheit. It is also the fullest season. The Baths moorings go early. White Bay fills at lunch. Christmas and New Year are not ordinary winter weeks. They book first and they price like it. If those dates matter, you inquire in summer, not in November.

November and May — the shoulders

Same warm water, fewer day boats, more room on the field. November can still carry early-season leftover weather. May is often the week we like for a family that wants The Baths without a tender traffic jam. Rates ease. Availability opens.

June through November — heat, rain, and honesty

Atlantic hurricane season runs June through November. Most weeks in the Virgins are still fine. Some are not. Mid-summer is hotter on the granite and quieter in the caves. We do not sell a September week as if it were February. If a named system is in the basin, the captain and the desk change the plan or we do not go.

What “best” means for this desk

For a first-time family on a crewed or captain-only week: January–April if you will pay peak, or May if you want space. For The Baths as the point of the trip: any month, but go at dawn — the calendar does not beat the clock. For Anegada: a settled window inside a peak or shoulder week, never a leftover swell.
